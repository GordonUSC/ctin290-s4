Every term here carries two videos. One teaches the term: it defines it and demonstrates it, usually by changing one thing at a time so you can watch what that one thing does. One shows the term working inside something we have watched together, so the word attaches to a film you already know rather than to a stranger's footage. Read them in that order. Learn it, then go find it.

The first four terms are your required rewatch, 20 minutes 53 seconds in total. The six below them are here because they came up in class and you will want them later; they are reference, not homework. Nobody is being asked to watch all 59 minutes in one week.

01

Depth of Field

The zone of sharp focus in an image; controlled by aperture, focal length, and distance. Deep DoF keeps foreground and background sharp; shallow DoF isolates subject.

Teaches it

Learn Depth of Field in 6 Minutes (With Real Examples)

Never fStop 6:25

Builds the idea on real shots rather than diagrams, and refocuses the same frame so you can watch attention move without the camera moving at all.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6DZiJrL_9tU

See it in something we watched

Sinners, Official Trailer

Coogler shot large format for deep focus, so the background keeps competing for your eye instead of dissolving away. Ask which is telling you where to look and which is trusting you to choose.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bKGxHflevuk

02

Dutch Angle

A tilted or canted frame, not level with the horizon; often signals unease, chaos, or subjective perspective.

Teaches it

Why movies tilt the camera like this

Vox 5:28

Takes the tilt across decades of films and asks what it is actually for, including where it fails and reads as cheap. A term is worth more with its misuse attached.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SHYfsYQDr6M

See it in something we watched

Spider-Verse, “It’s a leap of faith”

Around 2:30. The frame inverts on the leap. The whole meaning of the shot is in the tilt.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hpuS9bvhEEQ

03

Pan / Tilt / Boom

Camera movements: pan (horizontal turn), tilt (vertical turn), boom (vertical lift). Boom is also a microphone arm.

Teaches it

Film Glossary: Shot Movement (Dolly, Crane, Track, Zoom, Tilt, Pan)

J.D. Swerzenski 1:40

One hundred seconds, one move per caption, demonstrated on screen as it is named. It also covers dolly, crane and track, so it does duty for three more terms further down.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R994JUx7PJ8

See it in something we watched

Grand Theft Auto VI, Trailer 1

The clip you annotate in class, so the vocabulary and the activity point at the same ninety seconds.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QdBZY2fkU-0

04

Parallax

The apparent shift in position between foreground and background layers when camera moves; creates depth and dimensionality.

Teaches it

Walt Disney's MultiPlane Camera, filmed 13 Feb 1957

Walt Disney Productions, 1957 7:20

Walt Disney demonstrating the multiplane camera in 1957, on the actual machine, pulling painted glass layers apart to show why depth appears when the camera travels. It is the origin of the technique and still the clearest explanation of it ever filmed.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YdHTlUGN1zw

See it in something we watched

Spider-Verse, First 9 Minutes

0:00 to 9:00. The same trick seventy years later, done in a renderer. Watch the planes separate as the camera travels. A game engine hands you this for free.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zzH4rV08TLI

Also covered today Came up in the opening and in the annotation activity

05

Dolly

Moving the whole camera toward or away from the subject, rather than zooming. The relationship between foreground and background changes, which is why it feels like travel and a zoom does not.

Teaches it

The Difference Between Dolly and Zoom Shots

Film Riot 6:22

Runs the two side by side on the same subject, which is the only way this lands.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AKOxbCx1LNc

See it in something we watched

Sinners, Official Trailer

A dolly changes what is behind the subject. A zoom does not. Find one of each.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bKGxHflevuk

06

Crane / Boom move

Lifting or lowering the camera through space on an arm. Usually used to reveal scale, or to leave a scene rather than cut away from it.

Teaches it

The Art Of The CRANE SHOT

Tao Hudson 4:48

Spends its time on why a crane move is chosen rather than on the rig, which is the half that transfers to an engine. Your virtual camera has an infinite jib and no budget line.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SojAuaWQ0fk

See it in something we watched

Sinners, Official Trailer

Where the camera rises, ask what you are being shown that the character cannot see.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bKGxHflevuk

07

Focal length

How wide or narrow the lens sees. Wide lenses exaggerate depth and distance between planes; long lenses compress them and flatten the space.

Teaches it

What is Lens Compression? Camera Focal Length Explained

Remy Leonard 5:29

Holds the subject the same size and changes only the lens, so what you are watching is purely the background expanding and collapsing. That is compression isolated.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fC-fyXCAWzA

See it in something we watched

Coogler on Shooting With IMAX Film Cameras

Coogler talks format rather than lens, but it is the same idea: the choice is made before the shot and it decides what the shot can mean.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=okvFCZi5B0k

08

Tracking / Following shot

The camera travels with a moving subject, holding them roughly in frame. Keeps you attached to a character rather than watching them from outside.

Teaches it

How to Shoot Better Tracking Shots

StudioBinder 5:06

Separates a tracking shot from a pan that happens to follow someone, which is the distinction most people get wrong first.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mkVYpzyJvG8

See it in something we watched

Grand Theft Auto VI, Trailer 1

Ask who the camera is loyal to. A follow is a claim about whose story this is.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QdBZY2fkU-0

09

Handheld vs. locked-off

Handheld carries visible human motion; locked-off is fixed and still. One puts a body in the room with the subject, the other puts a witness at a distance.

Teaches it

Why Great Directors Lock the Camera

StudioBinder 11:57

Argues that stillness is a decision rather than an absence of one, using directors who lock the frame deliberately.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eL_XaKA5qWM

See it in something we watched

Sinners, Official Trailer

Stillness is a camera move. It is a decision not to move, and in an engine it costs exactly the same nothing as anything else.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bKGxHflevuk

10

Motivated camera move

A move that exists because something in the scene requires it: a subject moves, a fact needs revealing, an emotion turns. The opposite is decoration.

Teaches it

Motivated VS Unmotivated Camera Movement

Brickwall Pictures 5:07

Puts motivated and unmotivated versions of the same move next to each other. If you cannot get started on your sentences, start here.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DfhI4RV5KKQ

See it in something we watched

Grand Theft Auto VI, Trailer 1

One sentence per move saying what it is chasing. A move with no sentence is decoration, and finding those is the exercise.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QdBZY2fkU-0
